# Why Do Leaves Turn Yellow in the Fall?

*Last updated: 2025-01-15*

## The short answer

Leaves turn yellow in autumn because the tree **stops making and then dismantles its green pigment, chlorophyll** — and chlorophyll was masking **yellow-orange pigments called carotenoids that were present in the leaf all along.** When the green fades, the yellow that was hidden underneath becomes visible.

The tree isn't "turning yellow." It's *removing the green*, and the yellow is simply what's left.

---

## The three pigments involved

Leaves contain three main classes of pigment, and their relative amounts determine the color:

| Pigment | Color it produces | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Chlorophyll** (a and b) | Green | The pigment of photosynthesis; captures light energy |
| **Carotenoids** | Yellow, orange, brown | Accessory pigments; present throughout the growing season |
| **Anthocyanins** | Red, purple | Water-soluble; often *produced* fresh in autumn (not just unmasked) |

(US Forest Service, https://www.fs.usda.gov/visit/fall-colors/joy-and-science)

---

## The mechanism, step by step

1. **Trigger: shorter days and longer nights.** As autumn arrives, increasing night length is the key signal. As the US Forest Service puts it: *"As night length increases in the autumn, chlorophyll production in leaves slow down then is eventually destroyed. The carotenoids and anthocyanin that are present in the leaf are then unmasked and begin to show their colors."* (https://www.fs.usda.gov/visit/fall-colors/joy-and-science)

2. **Chlorophyll stops being made — and is actively broken down.** Harvard Forest notes chlorophyll is *"a large and expensive molecule to make"* (each ring contains four nitrogen atoms), so the tree reclaims it: *"Chlorophyll is normally broken down towards the end of the leaf life span, and much of the nitrogen is resorbed by the plant."* (Harvard Forest, https://harvardforest.fas.harvard.edu/education-opportunities/classic-outreach-resources/autumn-foliage-color/leaf-science/leaf-pigments)

3. **The yellow was always there.** Carotenoids are synthesized in the leaf's plastids during the growing season. They primarily absorb blue wavelengths, letting longer (yellow) wavelengths through. Harvard Forest: *"In autumn foliage, the carotenoids are left over in the chloroplasts and revealed from the loss of chlorophyll."* (https://harvardforest.fas.harvard.edu/education-opportunities/classic-outreach-resources/autumn-foliage-color/leaf-science/leaf-pigments)

4. **Then the leaf is shed.** The leaf is preparing to fall (the process is called *senescence*), which is why the color change and leaf-drop happen together. (Harvard Forest, https://harvardforest.fas.harvard.edu/education-opportunities/classic-outreach-resources/autumn-foliage-color/leaf-science/leaf-process)

---

## Why yellow is the "default" and red is different

There's an important distinction between **yellow** and **red** autumn leaves:

- **Yellow/orange leaves** (e.g., black maple, many birches, hickories) get their color from **carotenoids that were already in the leaf** — the color is *unmasked* when chlorophyll degrades.
- **Red/purple leaves** (e.g., red maple, sugar maple) get their color largely from **anthocyanins**, which are often **newly produced** in autumn rather than simply revealed. As the Royal Society of Chemistry explains, leaves *"produce more anthocyanin in colder, drier weather and accumulate more sugar in their cells when bright, sunny autumn days continue to drive photosynthesis through any remaining chlorophyll."* (RSC Education, https://edu.rsc.org/everyday-chemistry/the-chemistry-behind-leaves-changing-colour-and-falling-from-trees/4018155.article)

So "turning yellow" is fundamentally a *subtractive* process (green removed), whereas "turning red" is partly an *additive* one (new pigment made).

---

## Common misconceptions

- ❌ **"Cold weather turns leaves yellow."** Temperature affects the *intensity and duration* of color, but the primary trigger is **day/night length (photoperiod)**, which is why the timing is highly predictable. (Purdue Extension, https://www.purdue.edu/fnr/extension/autumn-leaves-what-influences-the-color/)
- ❌ **"The yellow pigment appears in fall."** It was there all summer — chlorophyll just overpowered it.
- ❌ **"Leaves change color because they're dying."** More precisely, the tree is *actively* recycling the leaf's valuable nitrogen and other nutrients before deliberately cutting it loose. The color change is part of a controlled, energy-saving shutdown, not passive decay.

---

## Summary

Leaves turn yellow because **chlorophyll — the green pigment — is broken down and its nitrogen reclaimed by the tree as days shorten**, revealing the **carotenoid pigments (yellow-orange) that were present all along.** Red and purple foliage works differently: those colors largely come from **anthocyanins newly synthesized in autumn**, especially under cold, dry, bright conditions.
