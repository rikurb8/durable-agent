# Battery Recycling: Market, Technologies, Recovery Rates, and Policy Drivers

*Last updated: 2025-01-15*

Battery recycling is the recovery of valuable metals and materials from spent batteries — increasingly lithium-ion (Li-ion) packs from electric vehicles (EVs), consumer electronics, and grid storage, alongside the long-established lead-acid recycling industry. This article summarizes the current market, dominant technologies, recovery rates, and the regulatory and economic forces shaping the sector.

## 2. Recycling technologies

Three main technology families dominate:

### Pyrometallurgical (smelting)
- Uses high-temperature smelting to recover metals. It is **cheaper and can handle almost any battery design or chemistry**, but **the heat drives off lithium and reduces its recovery rate**. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)
- A 2025 life-cycle assessment notes that even under the EU's targets, pyrometallurgical routes face limits on recovering lithium specifically. (Green Chemistry, RSC, https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/gc/d4gc05409a)

### Hydrometallurgical (chemical leaching)
- Leaches shredded "black mass" in acid to extract metal sulfates, lithium carbonate, and graphite. A key advantage is higher lithium recovery than pyrometallurgy.
- Example: the German firm **Duesenfeld** uses vacuum drying to remove solvent at low temperature (avoiding hydrogen-fluoride formation), then a hydrometallurgical process to recover metal sulfates, lithium carbonate, and graphite — recovering **91% of the mass of all materials in the battery**, per the company. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)

### Direct recycling (cathode-to-cathode)
- Recovers and "rejuvenates" cathode active material rather than breaking it down to elements. A 2025 study states **direct recycling can achieve a recovery rate of >98% using cathode rejuvenation**. (ScienceDirect, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378775325020828)

**Comparison summary:** Pyrometallurgy = robust but lithium-poor recovery; hydrometallurgy = good all-round recovery (especially lithium); direct recycling = highest recovery but most sensitive to feedstock purity/chemistry uniformity.

## 3. Recovery rates

### Current real-world collection/recycling rates
- The actual recovery rate of spent Li-ion batteries was **less than 5% globally in 2019**. (PMC / *Progress, Key Issues, and Future Prospects for Li-Ion Battery Recycling*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9749081)
- North American recycling rate was **lower than 10% in 2012**; Europe's reported collection rate was **slightly above 15% in 2016**. (PMC, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7040742)
- By contrast, **lead-acid batteries achieve roughly a 95% recycling rate worldwide** — a mature, economically self-sustaining system. (Worldmetrics, https://worldmetrics.org/battery-recycling-industry-statistics)

### Regulatory recovery targets (EU, Reg. 2023/1542)
Mandatory targets for lithium, cobalt, copper, lead, and nickel:
- **Lithium recovery:** 50% by end-2027 → 80% by end-2031
- **Cobalt, copper, lead, nickel recovery:** 90% by end-2027 → 95% by end-2031
- **Recycling efficiency:** 65% for lithium-based batteries by end-2025 (rising further by end-2030); 75% for lead-acid, 80% for nickel-cadmium by end-2025
- **Recycled content in new batteries** (from 18 Aug 2031): 16% cobalt, 85% lead, 6% lithium, 6% nickel
(EUR-Lex summary of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/LSU?uri=CELEX%3A32023R1542)

Note: some secondary sources cite slightly different phrasings of the recycling-efficiency targets (e.g., "at least 90% for cobalt, copper, nickel and lead; at least 50% for lithium"), but the EUR-Lex summary above is the authoritative version. (Flash Battery, https://www.flashbattery.tech/en/blog/eu-battery-regulation-obligations-updates)

### China
China has set **nonbinding targets to recover 98% of batteries' nickel, cobalt, and manganese and 85% of the lithium**, and has deployed regulations making domestic EV manufacturers responsible for recycling end-of-life batteries. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)

## 4. Industry capacity and feedstock

- More than **200 businesses** now have combined capacity to recycle **over 1 million metric tons of end-of-life batteries per year** (Circular Energy Storage, via C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover).
- Global recycling capacity reached **over 300 GWh/year** in 2024. (IEA, Global EV Outlook 2024, https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2024/outlook-for-battery-and-energy-demand)
- **Feedstock mismatch is the central structural problem:** battery-production **scrap** — not end-of-life batteries — currently accounts for much of the material processed. **End-of-life batteries won't become a major feedstock source until after 2030.** (McKinsey, via C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)
- Consequence: **"On a global basis, we have much more capacity than what we need,"** and much current capacity is underused while recyclers wait for EOL batteries. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)
- EOL volume outlook: around **1.2 million EV batteries could reach end of life in 2030, and 14 million in 2040**. (IEA, Battery Circularity, https://www.iea.org/reports/battery-circularity)

## 5. Key players

- **China:** CATL is China's largest battery maker *and* largest battery recycler, capable of recycling **120,000 metric tons per year**. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)
- **North America:** Redwood Materials, Cirba Solutions, Ascend Elements, and Li-Cycle are key players. (Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/the-companies-shaping-the-north-american-lithium-ion-recycling-ecosystem)
  - Redwood Materials' existing hydrometallurgical facility near Reno had capacity for **2,500 t/year** (per a US DOE environmental assessment), with plans to expand to 10,000 t and produce 20,000 t of cathode powder by 2026. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)
- **Europe:** Northvolt's **Revolt Ett** (Sweden) planned initial capacity of 8,500 t, expanding toward 125,000 t; Hydrovolt and others operate preprocessing facilities. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)

## 6. Economic drivers and challenges

### Drivers
- **Critical-mineral security:** Lithium, nickel, and cobalt are classified as critical by the EU due to economic importance and supply risk; recycling offers a domestic secondary supply. (ScienceDirect, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0956053X25003733)
- **Regulation:** The EU Battery Regulation is described as an unmatched global endorsement for recycling, mandating recovery targets *and* recycled-content requirements in new batteries. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)
- **US subsidies:** The US primarily uses economic instruments such as subsidies, exemplified by the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, to support domestic and allied recycling. (ScienceDirect, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352550926000035)
- **Environmental benefits:** Recycling can reduce the need for primary mining and lower the environmental impact of making new batteries. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)

### Challenges
- **Feedstock scarcity until ~2030** (see §4) leaves capacity underutilized. (C&EN, https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-10138-cover)
- **Weak black-mass pricing:** Inferred black mass prices for NCM/NCA (cif South Korea) peaked at **$6,590/tonne in August 2023** before falling to a low of **$4,033/tonne on February 14, 2024**. (Fastmarkets, https://www.fastmarkets.com/uploads/2024/05/Recycling_LTF_Q1_2024_Sample.pdf)
- **High feedstock costs and gate fees** in the US are pressuring recycling economics. (Fastmarkets, https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/us-battery-recyclers-remain-under-pressure-refiners-producers-squeezed-under-current-market-drivers)
- **Innovation is shifting:** International patenting related to battery circularity grew **42% per year on average from 2017–2023**, while growth in primary-ore refining is lower — signaling recycling is increasingly competitive with primary supply. (IEA/EPO, https://www.iea.org/reports/battery-circularity)
- **Caveat on EU targets:** One 2026 study argues the EU's recycled-content targets **cannot effectively drive domestic recycling without sufficient cathode production capacity** in Europe. (ScienceDirect, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352550926000035)

---

## 7. Summary

Battery recycling is a fast-growing but structurally young industry. Demand drivers (EVs, storage, mineral security, regulation) are strong, and Li-ion recycling technology is maturing across pyrometallurgical, hydrometallurgical, and direct-recycling routes. However, the sector faces a **timing mismatch**: capacity has been built ahead of feedstock, because most end-of-life batteries won't arrive in volume until after 2030, leaving plants underused and margins pressured by weak black-mass prices. Regulatory mandates — especially the EU Battery Regulation — are the most powerful force pushing recovery rates and recycled content upward, while lead-acid's ~95% recycling rate remains the benchmark that Li-ion recycling has not yet approached.
